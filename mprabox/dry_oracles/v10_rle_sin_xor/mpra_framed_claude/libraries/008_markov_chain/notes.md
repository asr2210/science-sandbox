# 008 — per-sequence Markov chain (dinucleotide-varying)

## Design
Each sequence generated by a Markov chain with per-sequence transition matrix sampled from Dirichlet(1,1,1,1). 200bp.

## Result
- eval_01 mean_r = **0.4903** — worse than random uniform (0.518)
- K562 r = 0.9584 (vs 0.994) — small drop
- HepG2 r = 0.5128 (vs 0.567) — clear drop
- SK-N-SH r ≈ 0 — flat

## Reading
The per-sequence Markov chain inadvertently broadened the GC distribution (std=0.134 with extremes from 0.005 to 0.995) because rows were Dirichlet(1,1,1,1) without GC stationary constraint.

But the more telling result is **HepG2 dropped to 0.513**. Even though K562 is mostly preserved (still 0.96), HepG2 lost ~0.05. This suggests HepG2 prediction is sensitive to dinucleotide structure in a way random uniform happens to give the model the right exposure for. Markov chain sequences with VARIED dinucleotide profiles are out-of-distribution for HepG2 prediction.

Combined with prior evidence, this strengthens the picture: **the eval distribution looks like uniform random DNA at the mononucleotide AND dinucleotide level**. Any structured deviation hurts.

## Implication
Stop trying compositional modifications. The path forward should test real MPRA-tested sequences (which have specific structures that may or may not match eval) and ultimately may need to accept random uniform as the best library on this benchmark.
